# V1 — CC0 packs

**Why.** The learner finds the world flat and empty; generic street things
(lamps, benches, bins, trees, traffic lights) are the cheapest way to fill
it — and nobody needs to hand-draw a bin. Beijing's own architecture stays
ours (V2).

**What was checked.** Each license page read on 2026-09-30:
- Kenney **RPG Urban Pack** — "Creative Commons CC0", 16×16; `License.txt`
  in the zip says CC0 1.0. **Used.**
- Kenney **Tiny Town** — CC0, but thick dark outlines on its ground tiles
  (the brief: no styles that fight). Not used.
- **Ninja Adventure** (pixel-boy) — the itch.io page answered 403, so the
  license could not be read. Unclear → not used.
- Kenney Roguelike Indoors / Modern City — CC0 on their pages; not
  downloaded (V2 draws our interiors and blocks).

**What the game gets.** 20 frames in the props atlas, recoloured into our
palette by `scripts/world/art/import-urban.ts` (per-frame letter swaps so
the trees wear our greens; the autumn trees became gold ginkgo 银杏):
`urban/*` (16×16) — bins red and grey, a bench, a hydrant, a produce crate,
a crate, hedge left/middle/right, a road barrier, two traffic lights;
`urban-tall/*` (16×32) — three street lamps (one the ornate green kind), a
bus-stop sign, a round and a small tree, a big and a small ginkgo. Review
sheet: `docs/world-game/review/v/urban-pack.png`. Credits in
`public/world/CREDITS.md`; the original sheet and license in
`content/world/art/vendor/kenney-rpg-urban/`.

**Not done here.** Placing them on the maps is V2/T/V5's work (each map is
redone or walked there, and every prop is checked in place). Cars were left
out (drawn from straight above).
