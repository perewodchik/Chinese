# 走走 — the story bible (§13 Z0)

The one place that holds the truth for §13. Every content task (K3, S1–S10,
U1–U4, L1–L3, N1, B-books) reads this first and updates it when it
decides something new. Facts live in `facts.md`; every real-world claim in a
line, a book or a note has a row there.

**The rule over everything (§13.0):** the learner wants to *hear, read and
say Chinese* in real Beijing, with a story good enough to come back
tomorrow — not a longer game. A beat earns its place by a Chinese line the
learner says or reads, or a piece of real culture they understand better.

## 1. The throughline — 团圆 (reunion)

### 1.1 The lantern
- The lantern in 王阿姨's yard is a **走马灯** (a revolving lantern: the
  candle's warm air turns an inner wheel, and the painted figures on it
  run round the paper shade). **王阿姨's grandfather, a lantern maker,**
  made and painted it for her when she was small. The content's line
  「灯笼是我奶奶的。」 changes once (S1) to 「灯笼是我爷爷做的。」 and the
  card's *knows* line to "…since her grandparents' time".
- Each spirit is one painted figure on the wheel. When the lantern broke
  (ch1), the figures ran off into the city. Each spirit you bring back
  **lights its figure**, and 王阿姨 remembers one thing (§1.3).
- **Ten places on the wheel:** nine spirits and one blank. The grandfather
  left the last one unpainted — "for whoever lights it next". It is
  painted on the Great Wall in the epilogue (§2, 尾声).

- **灶王爷, the twist:** he is the one figure nobody looked for, because
  he never ran — a kitchen god stays with his family. On 小年 (腊月二十三)
  he goes up to heaven to report on the household (book 16); on 除夕 he
  comes back, and his figure lights during 年夜饭. So when 小军 arrives
  (ch9) "every figure is lit but one", and that one is the kitchen god,
  away in heaven — the book tells you why and when he'll be back.
- **天官** (X11, 《天官赐福》 line) is *not* a lantern figure; it is the
  little shrine's own story. Its 元宵 ending and the lantern's are **one
  night** (S10): both shown, the shrine's first, then the lantern.

### 1.2 The family (three generations in one 四合院)
- **王阿姨** — grew up in this courtyard; her grandfather (爷爷, the
  lantern maker, died when she was young), her father (repainted the 门神
  every 春节), herself (worked as a primary-school teacher, now retired —
  new, fits "treats you like a nephew").
- **小军** — her son, 30-ish, an engineer working abroad (already in
  `story-wang`). Loved the lantern as a boy; hid under it from the
  firecrackers. Hasn't been home for 春节 for three years. `story-wang`
  (calling him) stays a side quest; **chapter 9 needs that call** and offers
  it again at its start if it hasn't happened.
- You — the tenant from far away who becomes family.

### 1.3 The memories (one per returned spirit, K2's memory cutscene, 2–4 lines, HSK 1–2)
Next time you are home after a spirit returns, 王阿姨 by the lantern:
1. **石狮子** — she as a little girl at 元宵; 爷爷 lit the lantern and the
   figures ran round; she laughed and chased them.「我小时候，爷爷每年都点这个灯。」
2. **九尾狐** — 爷爷 took her skating on 后海 in winter; he copied the
   fox from a picture book.「冬天，爷爷带我去后海滑冰。」
3. **门神** — her father repainted the door gods every 春节; she held the
   paint pot.「每年春节，爸爸画门神，我拿颜色。」
4. **麒麟** — she studied to be a teacher; 爷爷 said the qilin loves
   people who learn.「爷爷说：麒麟喜欢学习的人。」
5. **石猴** — at the 白云观 fair, little 小军 on her shoulders touched the
   stone monkey.「小军小时候，也摸过石猴。」
6. **貔貅** — the day 小军 left to work abroad: "I'll be home for 春节."
   Then three years.「他说春节回来……三年了。」 (the one sad line — short;
   兔儿爷 answers with a joke right after)
7. **年兽** — 年夜饭 years ago: 小军 hid under the lantern from the
   firecrackers.「小军怕鞭炮，在灯下面不出来。」
8. **龙** — 爷爷's last lantern: he painted the dragon's eyes last. Then
   her phone rings: 小军 is coming home for 春节 (the end of ch8).
9. **灶王爷** — on 除夕, at the table:「灶王爷看了我们家一百年。」

### 1.4 兔儿爷's own small arc
He is a 中秋 clay rabbit (a real Beijing folk toy, facts.md) who sat on the
lantern as its little guardian; the lantern breaking woke him (「我也在灯笼里。」,
K3). He fears
that when the lantern is whole the magic ends and he goes back to clay.
It surfaces three times, lightly: after ch4 ("…what happens to me when it's
whole?"), in ch8 after 画龙点睛, and on 元宵 — he doesn't go back:
王阿姨 puts him on the windowsill "where he belongs", and he winks.
Never sad for more than one line; the next line is always a joke.

## 4. Conventions for §13 content
- **Substory quests:** id `sub-<who>-<n>` (`sub-hu-1`, `sub-mn-3`,
  `sub-tt-2`, `sub-misha-4`), `kind: 'side'`; the coverage script counts
  them as substory episodes by the `sub-` prefix.
- **New main steps** get new ids; old step ids stay (S1's save upgrade).
- **New scenes, flags and cutscenes of chapter N** are prefixed `c<N>-` (`c1-list`, `c1-drum`); how to add steps, books and cutscenes, the hand-off contract and the chapter-9 hooks are in `s13/S-howto.md`.
- **Cutscene ids:** `<chapter>-open`, `<chapter>-finale`,
  `spirit-return-<spirit>`, `memory-<n>`, `sub-<who>-<n>-<beat>`.
- **Books:** `content/world/books/<id>.json`, id = pinyin of the title
  (`denglong`, `shishizi`, `chenzhong-mugu` …).
- **One sad line at most per scene**, followed by warmth or a joke.
- **Facts:** anything real in a line gets a row in `facts.md` first.
